According to legend, Rome was founded in 753 BC by Romulus, who became its first
king after killing his twin brother Remus.

Rome was ruled by seven kings from 753 BC to 509 BC, ending when the last king,
Tarquin the Proud, was overthrown and the Roman Republic was established.

The Roman Republic began in 509 BC, governed by elected consuls and a Senate
rather than a king.

Rome fought three wars against Carthage called the Punic Wars, from 264 to 146 BC.
The Second Punic War (218-201 BC) featured Hannibal's famous crossing of the Alps
with war elephants. Rome ultimately destroyed Carthage in 146 BC.

Rome conquered Greece in the 2nd century BC, absorbing Greek culture, art, and
philosophy into Roman society.

Julius Caesar rose to power as a military general, conquering Gaul (modern France)
between 58-50 BC. He crossed the Rubicon River in 49 BC, starting a civil war, and
became dictator of Rome.

Julius Caesar was assassinated on the Ides of March, 44 BC, by a group of senators
including Brutus and Cassius, who feared he wanted to become king.

After Caesar's death, civil war broke out between Mark Antony and Octavian
(Caesar's adopted heir). Octavian defeated Antony and Cleopatra at the Battle of
Actium in 31 BC.

In 27 BC, Octavian became Rome's first emperor and took the name Augustus,
marking the end of the Republic and the start of the Roman Empire.

The Pax Romana ("Roman Peace") was a roughly 200-year period of relative peace
and stability across the Roman Empire, lasting from 27 BC to 180 AD.

In 64 AD, a great fire destroyed much of Rome under Emperor Nero, who was
rumored (likely unfairly) to have started it himself.

The Colosseum was completed in 80 AD under Emperor Titus, built for gladiator
contests and public spectacles.

From 96 to 180 AD, Rome was ruled by five emperors — Nerva, Trajan, Hadrian,
Antoninus Pius, and Marcus Aurelius — considered some of its most stable and
effective leaders, often called the "Five Good Emperors."

Between 235-284 AD, Rome faced a period of civil war, invasion, and economic
collapse known as the Crisis of the Third Century, with emperors rising and
falling rapidly.

Emperor Diocletian split the Empire into Eastern and Western halves in 285 AD
to make it easier to govern and defend.

Emperor Constantine legalized Christianity in 313 AD with the Edict of Milan,
and it later became the empire's dominant religion.

Constantine founded the city of Constantinople in 330 AD as a new capital for
the Eastern Roman Empire.

The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 AD when the Germanic leader Odoacer
deposed the last Roman emperor, Romulus Augustulus.

The Eastern Roman Empire, later known as the Byzantine Empire, continued for
nearly another thousand years until it fell to the Ottoman Turks in 1453 AD.
